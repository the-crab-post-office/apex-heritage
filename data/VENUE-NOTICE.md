# Trackside furniture

`venue.json` contains original, authored prototype barrier and grandstand placements, not mapped, scanned or surveyed real facilities. They are deliberately editable approximations, generated by `scripts/build-venue.py` using the licensed circuit, pit and building databases already distributed with the game (see their notices). No photographs, official sponsor graphics, team logos or grandstand assets were copied.

All ten circuits receive Armco and/or concrete barriers, stacked tyres, simplified catch fencing and six tiered spectator stands. The generator uses the entire closed race route, open mapped pit route and mapped building polygons as exclusions: race/pit half-width plus 3.5 metres; buildings plus 1 metre. Tunnel sections are omitted. This protects legitimate pit entry/exit joins; circuit shoulders and terrain remain approximate. Stand roofs and seats are simplified original geometry. This is not an FIA safety/safety-engineering model.

Shared Rust collision uses barrier capsules and a one-metre car-centre radius, swept in at most 0.4-metre steps. Tyres are more compliant than concrete/Armco. Impacts reduce velocity and invalidate the current lap; there is no deformable barrier or damage simulation. Grandstands are scenery, not additional collision bodies.

Regenerate offline: install Python `shapely`, then run `python3 scripts/build-venue.py` from the repository root. Stand footprints include roof overhangs. Exact polygon/capsule exclusions and byte-deterministic regeneration are covered by `tests/test_venue.py`; Rust additionally checks route clearance, swept impacts and all pit-route points.
