# Corner surfaces and venue furniture — preview scope and attribution

**0.9 local exception/update:** Eau Rouge/Raidillon now uses a geographically anchored, photo-guided modern climb and the same local kerbs/paved apron/barriers for all eras, explicitly requested by the user. 1967 and 2023 car silhouettes are revised with shared web/native meshes. These are authored approximations, not surveys or scans. See [PHOTO-REFERENCE-NOTICE.md](PHOTO-REFERENCE-NOTICE.md); the remaining historical-layout limitations below still apply.

**This preview does not fulfil the requested full historical layouts.** All ten circuits have corner-specific kerb/runoff profiles for the four car eras, but the three historical profiles use the supported recent road alignment. Setup and race HUD explicitly say `RECENT-LAYOUT SUBSTITUTE`. This is not a valid claim that every older circuit was unavailable. In particular, historic Spa, Silverstone, Hockenheim and Interlagos had substantially different roads. Grandstands, buildings, pits, starts, elevation, AI and minimaps still use recent mapped geometry. Montréal 1967 and Nürburgring GP 1967 are separately explained as nonexistent-era combinations; that exception does not justify the other unfinished layouts.

## What was actually referenced

`corner-details.json` records source URLs, reference observations, dated reference inventories, inspected photo links and per-zone approximation notes. Aerial photographs were inspected for all ten circuits. For Spa, Monaco, Monza, Silverstone, Suzuka, Interlagos, Imola and Montréal, the inspected recent satellite photographs are linked from [F1 circuits from space](https://oversteer48.com/f1-circuits-from-space/) (page updated 7 February 2024; image capture dates not established here). Hockenheim reference views: [Motodrom album](https://fly-foto.de/luftbild/album/hockenheim/) and [main stand, 24 August 2017](https://fly-foto.de/luftbild/haupttribuene-des-hockenheimring-baden-wuerttemberg-2017-08-24-img-102548-jpg/). Nürburgring: [2016 GP facilities photograph](https://www.kuladig.de/Objektansicht/O-84193-20140128-3) and [page reproducing a 2004 aerial](https://inlandstourismus.de/der-nuerburgring). Their photographs are reference-only and are **not redistributed or used as textures**. Earlier Wikipedia file inventories are additional reference leads, not a claim that every image was inspected or licensed.

Actual grandstand footprints, gravel polygons and barrier-line candidates derive from OpenStreetMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors, [ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/), [copyright/attribution](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). The November 2026 source feature snapshot is `venue-osm.json`; its source and the adapted `corner-details.json` are provided as editable databases, including in the web build's `/licenses/`. Existing road/pit/building source notices still apply. These are mixed-date reference approximations, **not a 2023 survey**, laser scan or historical photogrammetry reconstruction.

Mapped stand polygons are represented by oriented rectangles and original simplified tiers/seats/roofs. Matching OSM buildings are not extruded a second time. Monaco temporary stands have no useful mapped grandstand polygons in this snapshot: the two harbour-side platforms are approximate photo-area anchors, not measured temporary structures. Existing OSM barriers include ordinary fences/walls; they are proximity-filtered collision proxies, **not individually verified race-safety installations**. Tyre rows are approximate authored rows at selected named escape-area ends, not observed exact stack positions.

## Kerbs and runoff

Named corner windows were authored against circuit diagrams and inspected photographs. Only selected windows receive runoff; there is no full-lap generic gravel shoulder. Modern mapped gravel polygons replace overlapping authored strips. Only closed areas are eligible; mapped roads/paths, parking, parks, sport courts and beaches are rejected instead of turning every gravel/sand feature into a race escape area. Widths, ramp/ridge profiles, heights, historic material choices and escape-area extents are gameplay estimates, **not photo-measured engineering dimensions**. A single outside-side estimate across an S-bend may remain inaccurate. Exact shared clipped triangles are used by Rust contact, the web renderer and native renderer; wheel support and floor probes do not collide with an invisible separate kerb shape.

Four oriented tyre contact patches are tested against the road's outer white-line boundary. A tyre touching the boundary is still legal. `offtrack` requires zero of the four patches to overlap; surface grip is sampled separately across each patch, so a still-valid car can lose grip. Kerbs outside the white line do not themselves extend track limits. Pit-lane travel retains the separate permitted pit state. The ride-height/spring/floor-strike model is intentionally approximate, not a validated suspension or rigid-body solver. Raised ridges can strike the floor without all four wheels leaving the track; strikes produce drag, heave reaction, sparks and a HUD warning.

## Rebuilding

Install Python 3 and Shapely, then run `python3 scripts/build-corner-details.py`. It is deterministic and offline, consuming the checked-in routes, building footprints, pit lanes and feature snapshot. No copyrighted reference photographs are required to run or rebuild the game. Full historical routing and verified historic structure placement remain future work; do not remove the substitute labels until the associated road, pits, start, elevation, AI, scenery and minimap have actually been reconstructed together.
